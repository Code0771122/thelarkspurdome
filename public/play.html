How to play

This is a traditional murder-mystery party. Put the phone down and talk to each other. The phone is only your rulebook, your character booklet, your alibi and your notebook.

  1. Be your character. Read Your part, Secret and People before the first round. Dress the part, use the voice, keep the habits all night.
  2. Never break character. From the moment you arrive until the truth is revealed, you are your character: the voice, the manners, the opinions. Anyone caught slipping out of character owes the room a forfeit, chosen by the host.
  3. Reveal and hide. At the start of every round your phone buzzes with Reveal this now: something your character must tell the room that round, in your own words. Everything on your Secret tab stays hidden unless you are caught.
  4. Introduce yourself. When the host calls your name, stand up and say your opening line.
  5. Know your alibi. It is on the Alibi tab, part of your story. Learn it and stick to it: from round IV it is your sworn statement.
  6. Work together. The household is a team. Share what you have seen, check each other's alibis and pool the clues. If most of the room names the killer, the household wins together. The killer wins alone if the vote goes wrong.
  7. Clues are read aloud. Each round the host opens envelopes on the big screen and reads them to the room. Listen carefully. Nothing appears on your phone.
  8. Question everyone, face to face. Ask where they were, who they saw, what they gain. Whisper in corners, form alliances, trade what you know.
  9. The one rule of lying. You may lie about your secret and your night. You may never lie about a clue that has been read out.
  10. The hot seat. The host may put someone in the hot seat: two minutes of questions from the room, answered in character. The room decides for itself who is lying.
  11. Motive, means, opportunity. The killer is the only guest who had all three: a reason to want him dead, a way to do it, and the time to do it. Keep notes on the Notes tab for every guest. Watch for red herrings: plenty of people look guilty.
  12. Listen to Corvin. Corvin saw everything from the dome and knows who did it. He will never say the name, but each round he whispers where to look.
  13. The accusation. In the last round every guest stands and makes an accusation speech: who did it, how and why, in thirty seconds. Then the Accuse tab opens and everyone votes in secret. Then the host breaks the seal.
Points

Naming the killer, the method, the motive, the time and the proof all score. So do your own goals, and the best performance of the night. If you are the murderer, you score by getting away with it.
